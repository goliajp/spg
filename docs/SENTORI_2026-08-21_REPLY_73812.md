## 1. What changed

`traits @> '…' AND received_at >= … AND received_at < …` uses both of
your indexes at once. Before, it used one: the containment was
answered by the GIN index, its rows arrived from the index and never
reached a scan, so the BRIN summaries had nothing to prune.

The two meet at the **row locator** — the moment after the seek hands
back which rows matched and before those rows are materialised. That
is the only point where both facts exist at the same time. A locator
whose slot the BRIN summary already ruled out cannot satisfy the range
predicate, so it is dropped there rather than fetched and re-checked.
This is the bitmap intersection PostgreSQL does, done at the locator
level.

One detail decided whether this worked at all: the slots are computed
from the **whole** predicate at the top call, not from the
sub-expression the seek recursion is descending into. The range that
prunes lives in a different conjunct from the containment that seeks —
compute it too far down and it is never in scope.

`None` still means "no opinion", as in 7.38.11: with no BRIN index, or
no usable bound, every locator is kept and the path is byte-identical
to before.

Over the wire, 200,000 rows:

```
SELECT count(*) … WHERE traits @> '…' AND received_at >= … AND < …
    9-11 ms  ->  2.119     (PostgreSQL 18: 1.8-2.0)
```

## 3. How we convinced ourselves it is not dropping rows

Intersecting two indexes is exactly the kind of change where a bug is
*faster* than the correct answer, so the checks are about the answer.

- **Thirteen corpus cases, every expectation taken from PostgreSQL
  18.** That mattered here: our first draft hand-computed 20 rows for
  a half-open one-hour window and the run answered 19. The
  implementation was right and our arithmetic was wrong. We take the
  oracle's number, not our own.
- **Watched failing.** Shortening each kept range by a single slot
  turns two cases red, and the failure mode is the one that matters —
  rows silently vanishing: 1000 becomes 999, 1 becomes 0.
- **Your own data, on this build.** The `kill -9` fixture built from
  your dump ran in this release's battery on two machines. Two of its
  three index probes are the two index kinds this change touches — the
  GIN on `device_tokens.traits` and the BRIN on `events.received_at` —
  and the rule is that the index-backed answer must agree row-for-row
  with a sequential scan of the same predicate, on a database reopened
  after `SIGKILL`. They agree. To be exact about what that does and
  does not prove: those two probes are on different tables, so neither
  one exercises the intersection itself. They establish that this build
  did not disturb either index on your data; the intersection is what
  the thirteen corpus cases above are for.
