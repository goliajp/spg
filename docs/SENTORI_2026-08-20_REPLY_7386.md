## One correction: `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` on a partial unique index was still open

Your §3 records it as fixed in 7.38.4. It was not. We ran
`on-conflict-partial-index.sql` against the published 7.38.4 binary and
case B still raised:

```
B: PARTIAL unique index, untargeted DO NOTHING   expect INSERT 0 0
ERROR:  duplicate key value violates unique constraint "oc_partial"
```

We do not know how the row got its tick — whether the suite stopped
needing the shape, or the line moved when the table was edited — and it
does not much matter. What matters is the shape of the mistake: **an
entry that says closed while the defect is open is worse than one that
says open**, because it removes the case from everyone's attention at
once. Yours is the ledger we have been reading to decide what to work
on; we would rather flag this than let it stand.

**It is fixed in 7.38.6.** The bare clause collected its arbiters from
every unique constraint and every FULL unique index — the filter
required no partial predicate — so a conflict on a partial unique index
was invisible to `DO NOTHING` and escaped to the duplicate-key check.
Now an arbiter carries its predicate, and the predicate decides who is
in play on both sides: a row it rejects is not in the index, so it
neither conflicts nor blocks anything else, in the table or within one
statement. All three cases in your repro, and the predicate-false
cases, measured against PG18.

## Why the version number jumped: 7.38.5 was tagged and never published

Its own release gate went red — on `plan_cache`, one of our perf gates,
not on anything in the product. That gate compares two timed loops run
one after the other where the numerator is about 2 µs of work, so load
landing on one loop and not the other moved the verdict. There was no
regression behind it (0.117–0.258 on that tree, 0.141–0.176 on the
previous tag, spreads overlapping).

We fixed the instrument rather than the threshold — each round now runs
both batches, flipping which goes first, and the verdict is the median
of the per-round ratios; the 0.33 bound is unchanged. It then passed
*inside a release train*, which is the condition it had failed under:
0.152.

The tag `v7.38.5` stays where it is with nothing published under it. We
do not move a pushed tag, so the version number simply skips. If you
have anything pinned to a range, 7.38.5 will never resolve.

## Also in 7.38.6: a fast release path

You have twice been blocked for the days between finding a defect and
our next release. `scripts/release.sh --fast` exists for that: same
preflight and same artefacts, with the ~40-minute battery replaced by
our 150-second precommit smoke. It announces what it did not run, and
its checklist carries the debt rather than a green list.

We are telling you because it changes what a version number means. A
build shipped that way has had a smoke test, not a release battery, and
we will say so in the notes when we use it. If you would rather we never
used it for anything you run, say so and we will not.
